# Dublin 2026-11-27: PF1, Polymarket-first from Dublin (offline), Part B

_Status: STOPPED AT V0, as the brief says to when V0 fails. Branch `research/pf1`. No orders, no keys, no services touched._

## 1. Verdict

1. **V0 fails as specified, so PF1 was not judged.** The parameters were Lighter match at send + 300 ms and Polymarket match at send + 265 ms. On 11-26 the simulated pair P&L on live-paired triggers is **−0.19 ¢/share against −2.69 live**, a 2.5 ¢ gap where the tolerance is 1.5 ¢.
   - 11-27 passes: −1.80 vs −0.93.
   - Slippage passes both days: sim 8.0 / 8.5 ¢ vs live 9.0 / 7.5 ¢.
2. **Why: the simulator is too fast and too lucky on the Polymarket leg.**
   - Live Polymarket FAK acks take 402–456 ms (p10–p50, p90 ~740 ms). A match at send + 265 ms is earlier than nearly any live order could have matched.
   - Example, `1795698900-a-1795699620552`: the sim takes the 5 Down shares at 0.34 at +265 ms. Dublin's trade feed shows another taker bought exactly those 5 shares at exchange time +1738 ms, about 90 ms after our simulated match. The live order missed them and chased to 0.61 over 4 attempts.
   - The strict "displayed size" fill model has no queue or competitor race, so it pays the trigger price whenever the book still shows it at the simulated match time.
3. **Recalibrated on the validation days, V0 passes narrowly. This is not acted on.**
   - Settings: Lighter match at send + 161 ms (the measured exchange transaction time) and Polymarket match at send + 350–430 ms (the live ack median).
   - Result: 11-26 sim −1.27 vs live −2.69, a 1.42 ¢ gap. 11-27 −1.52 vs −0.93, a 0.59 ¢ gap. Slippage 9.0 / 8.0 vs 9.0 / 7.5.
   - Still optimistic on 11-26 by 1.4 ¢. 80% of that gap is one trigger (`1795716000-a-1795716821246`): the sim filled at +33.6 ¢ where live paid 45 ¢ of slippage and got −10.9 ¢.
4. **What this implies for PF1.**
   - The live Tokyo latencies imply a Polymarket match about 320 ms after the order reaches London (430 − ~110 ms transit), not the 150 ms `live` scenario hold.
   - Lighter's taker hold looks closer to ~160 ms than 300 ms.
   - A PF1 run with a 150 ms Polymarket hold would credit Dublin with a speed the live data do not show. The 250 ms alternative is the closer of the brief's two values.
   - The fill model also needs a competitor term, for example dropping displayed size that Dublin's trade feed shows was taken before our match plus a margin.
5. **Nothing after V0 was run.**
   - Not run: V2, the dev grid, the pick, the validation days.
   - `frozen/PF1_plan.json` is committed as written but unused. No `PF1_pick.json` exists. No holdout day was opened.

## 2. Decision needed

Pick one of these:
- **(A) Accept the recalibrated simulator and rerun PF1.** Settings: Lighter match send + 161 ms; Polymarket Tokyo match send + 430 ms, which implies a Polymarket hold of ~320 ms for Dublin; 250 ms as the fast alternative instead of 150.
  - V0 would be re-registered with these values, and the plan amended and pushed before any dev result is read.
  - Caveat: these two values were fitted on 11-26/27, the validation days. The V0 gate then only checks consistency, not out-of-sample fit.
- **(B) Add a competitor/queue term to the Polymarket fill first, then rerun V0.** It would remove displayed size that recorded trades took within ±X ms of our match.
- **(C) Close PF1.** On the evidence so far, the Tokyo losses came from the Polymarket leg being slow and raced. PF1 only helps if Dublin really matches ~250 ms sooner than Tokyo did.

## 4. V0: Tokyo probe replay (11-26/27)

- Persistence check at +250 ms, and the +500 ms display check.
- Tokyo views: Lighter = exchange + 2 ms; Polymarket = exchange + 112 ms.
- Lighter IOC at +500 ms at the trigger price, same size as live, matched after the hold.
- Polymarket FAK at the Lighter fill (known at +330 ms, sent +1.6 ms later), limit = ask + 3 ¢, matched at send + X.
