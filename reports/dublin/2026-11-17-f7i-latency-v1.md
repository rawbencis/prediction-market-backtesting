# F7i under live-measured latency — 2026-11-17

Frozen picks F7i-A and F7i-B (`frozen/F7i.json`, unchanged since 2026-11-16) rerun on dev (11-14, 11-15) and holdout (11-16) with two scenarios measured live on dublin 2, next to the existing dublin / dublin_p90 / tokyo rows. No rule or threshold was changed, and no other configuration was tried.

## Bottom line

- **live_hold150 is identical to dublin**: same rows, fills, settle and 1 s markout on every day and in both fill models. In F7i the taker hold only enters the exit-plan column (`exit_c`), which is not part of the pick. The 150 ms hold therefore doesn't affect these results. It would matter for any taker leg.
- **live_ack (order +200 ms, cancel +130 ms) cuts fills by ~85%**: B goes from 7,415 to 987 fills/day on holdout, and A from 1,008 to 104. At face value settle c/share holds up (B holdout +2.63¢ [+0.26, +4.97]), but this row is optimistic. In the sim, most cancels land before the order itself is on the book: the median order lifetime is −21 to −3 ms. A real cancel needs the order ID from the ack, so it can't land before about on-book + 130 ms.
- **With that constraint (`+ackc`), the speed edge disappears at live_ack latency:**
  - The 1 s markout falls to about zero or below: B is −0.42¢ on holdout and −0.03¢ on dev; A is +0.08¢ on holdout.
  - Settle CIs include 0 for B on dev and holdout, and for A on holdout. B holdout is +1.21¢ [−0.42, +2.74] on ~2,300 fills/day.
  - A on dev (+4.11¢ [+1.30, +6.90]) is the only ackc settle row clearly above 0, but its markout is only +0.56¢.
- The same constraint at dublin latency changes little: B holdout goes from +1.85 to +1.80¢ and A from +3.52 to +3.08¢. **The candidate survives only if live order acks are close to the 22 ms probe figure, not 200 ms.**
- **Implication for R-D4 / R-D5:** the shadow as registered assumes the dublin scenario. At the latency measured live on dublin 2, F7i isn't a demonstrated edge. Either fix the order path (ack ~200 ms vs the probe's 22 ms send→visible) or register the shadow with `live_ack+ackc` as the primary scenario. The live ack number determines whether this pick is a candidate.

## Reading it

1. **The 150 ms hold doesn't matter for F7i.** live_hold150 matched dublin row for row on all three days (326k / 433k / 417k rows; 0 differences in `filled`, `settle_c` or `mo1000`). Only `exit_c` differed, in 3–5k rows per day, because its taker exit is held. Any future F7i variant with a taker exit, and every taker family (F1/F4/F6), would need to be re-checked at 150 ms.
2. **The 200 ms order latency is what hurts.** The improve order must still be alone and inside the spread when it lands. At 200 ms most such levels are already improved past or pulled by the time we arrive, so fill rate drops from 0.25 to 0.04–0.06 (B).
3. **The cancel has to wait for the ack.** The alt-book pull fires about alt row + 9 ms. Without the ack constraint, that cancel "lands" 139 ms after the alt move, often before our order would be on the book at +209 ms. The sim then treats the order as never live, which removes most of the adverse cases. That is why plain live_ack looks better per fill than dublin. Requiring cancel ≥ on-book + 130 ms makes these orders rest for ~130 ms after the alt warning. Fills then rise to 2,300–3,300/day, and they are the adverse ones: 1 s markout goes to ≈0 or negative, and settle loses significance.
4. **The fill-model choice is second-order.** strict and dsim differ by ≤ 0.4¢/share in every row.
5. **Caveats from the overnight report still apply.** Holdout is one day. Our order is a phantom absent from the recorded book, so other bots' reactions aren't modelled. Settle P&L on 11-17 00–05 h did not replicate (not reread here).

## Config count / honesty

- New simulation runs: 2 new scenarios × 3 days × 2 fill models = 12, plus the `+ackc` variant for live_ack and dublin = 12. That is 24 runs, each producing the frozen picks (and the other frozen thr/rules rows in the file).
- No new picks, thresholds or rules were searched. The `+ackc` variant is a sim-realism constraint, not a strategy change, and was added after seeing that live_ack orders were "cancelled before arrival". Treat its addition as post-hoc, but it can only make results more conservative.

## Data hygiene and safety

- **No day after 11-16 was read.** `pmlat.hours` normally reads hour h+1, so 11-16 hour 23 would touch the 11-17 00h files. All new runs set `PMLAT_MAXDAY=2026-11-16`, which hard-blocks any later recording day. live_hold150 matching dublin row for row on 11-16 shows that the old dublin 11-16 rows didn't depend on 11-17 data either.
- Every job ran one at a time under `systemd-run --user --scope -p MemoryMax=800M -p MemorySwapMax=0`, behind the `guard.sh` gate. Available memory stayed ≥ 1.6 GB, and no job was killed.
- No orders were placed, cancelled or signed, no credentials or `.env` were read, and no service was touched.
