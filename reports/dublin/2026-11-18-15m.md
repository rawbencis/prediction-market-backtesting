## 1. Verdict
1. **Nothing on BTC 15m is worth real orders at `live` speed.** No maker rule tried has a positive 1 s markout or a settle CI above 0. That covers ≈ 216 dev cells over 12 families. Both frozen holdout checks on 11-17 fail.
2. **The 15m book is easier to rest in but no easier to profit from.** A joined level is passed in 1.0 s (vs 0.33 s on 5m), but resting fills are adversely selected: −0.9 to −1.5¢ at 1 s at any speed. That is 3–5× the 0.25–0.35¢ rebate.
3. **The one real effect is a speed effect we don't have.** F7i-A at Dublin-median speed earns +1.1¢ at 1 s on 15m (a third of its 5m size). At `live` speed (post +190 ms) it turns negative, on dev and on the 11-17 holdout.
4. **Leads exist but don't pay.** The alt books lead BTC 15m by ~310 ms, enough for a 190 ms order, but only ~25% of alt moves follow through. BTC 15m moves before BTC 5m, not after.
5. **The census makers who do earn on 15m (~+3¢/$) can't be copied by our sim.** Every simulated version of their "always at the best bid" behaviour loses. The likely gap is queue position from being permanently present, which only live orders can measure.

**Conclusion (Part 1).**
- **Resting is easier on 15m.** Its book is about 9× quieter than 5m's before the last 5 minutes (38 vs 330–360 prints/min), and the spread is ≤ 1¢ 96% of the time. Once we join a new level, the best bid takes a median 1.0 s to move one tick past us (5m: 0.33 s) and 3.5 s for two ticks (5m: 1.1 s). That is 3× more durable than 5m.
- **Reacting is no easier.**
  - A new best level still lasts only ~0.2 s, as on 5m.
  - Top-of-book imbalance ≥ 0.6 falls back below 0 within a median 175 ms. An imbalance seen at decision time is mostly gone when a 190 ms order lands.
  - Every alt-book pull signal fires 8–12 times a minute. A resting order survives 60 s without a signal only 2–18% of the time (best: 3 alt 15m books moving 1¢, 18%).
- **The alt books do lead BTC 15m:** median ~310 ms, alts first in 82% of matched moves, more than 190 ms ahead in 62%. But only about a quarter of alt moves are followed by a BTC 15m move within 1 s.
- **BTC 15m moves slightly before BTC 5m** (median 68 ms), so the 5m book isn't a useful lead for 15m.
- **Load and receive delay** are like 5m: p99 264 rows/s; receive delay p50 10 ms, p90 32 ms in the busiest seconds.
- **So: can an order that lands in 190 ms and cancels in 90 ms rest usefully here?** Yes, if it doesn't need to react. Passive resting at the best bid survives seconds instead of a third of a second. Any reactive pull fires before the order has done any work, and the alt lead is long enough for an alt-triggered entry but noisy.

## 4. Part 3 — ideas for a longer window

### 3a. Sibling books (15m vs the concurrent 5m in the last 5 minutes)
- **Mechanism:** both settle on the same 60 s Chainlink TWAP at the same instant. Strikes are the TWAP at each window's start, read from `~/feeds-rec` (the rule confirmed 83/83 on 11-15).
- **The test:** the market with the lower strike can't have the lower UP price. A violation = the other market's UP bid > this market's UP ask, so buying both ends pays ≥ $1 in every outcome.
- **Coverage:** 1-s samples, 11-15 and 11-16. 37 of 192 windows were skipped for TWAP gaps at a strike second.

| day | windows checked | samples | samples with a violation | of those, > both taker fees | windows involved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 11-15 | 68 | 13,661 | 28 (0.20%) | 10 | 9 |
| 11-16 | 87 | 14,963 | 15 (0.10%) | 3 | 3 |

- **Size:** apart from one sample, violations net of fees are 0.2–3.8¢ for 1–3 s. The exception is 11-15 E=1794771000: 14¢ for one second, with strikes $19 apart.
- **Why it's unusable:** strikes this close need the exact TWAP value at the start second. A 1-s difference in the strike reading flips the sign. A riskless trade would need certainty about the strike, which our recording (≤ 2 s tolerance, with gaps) can't give.
- **Closed:** rare (≈ 0.15% of seconds), tiny, and it depends on strike precision.
- **As a lead signal:** Part 1 shows BTC 15m moves before BTC 5m (median 68 ms), so the 5m book isn't a lead for the 15m maker. That use is covered by F7i `b5a5` (2b) and is no better than alt15.

## 7. What could not be tested, and what would be needed
- **Our own orders in the book.** The calibration against live F7i shows the sim lets ~13% of orders land alone at a new price. Live, 270/276 had someone at our price first.
  - All maker numbers here are upper bounds.
  - Only live 5-share orders, or recording our own order IDs against the book, can measure queue position and reactions.
- **Book open before the recorder:** the recorder opens 15m books 45 s before the window, so whether the 15m book is open earlier (and who sits at the front) is unknown. Needed: subscribe at market creation (Gamma `startDate`).
- **Depth:** updown-rec has only the UP top of book. The pess queue for a level that becomes best later is unknown until it is top. feeds-rec has 5 levels for BTC 5m/15m only.
- **TWAP strikes to the second:** the sibling no-arb check needs the exact TWAP value at each start second. feeds-rec's RTDS feed had gaps (37 of 192 windows unusable) and gives values to the second only within ±2 s.
- **Holdout size:** one day (11-17, ~96 BTC 15m windows). Even a real 1¢ edge couldn't be told from zero on settlement in one day; markouts are the powered measure.
- **Alchemy-key items:** none were needed.
- **ETH/SOL/XRP/DOGE 15m as targets (3h):** not run, because nothing worked on BTC.
- **Placebo pulls:** not rerun on 15m, because no reactive pull survived at live speed; the 5m placebo result stands.
