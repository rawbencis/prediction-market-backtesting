Data: recorded only. Development 2026-11-14 (from 09:00) – 11-16; holdout 2026-11-17, opened once after `frozen/late_dump.json` was committed (a8cc38e). Code: `~/overnight-wt` (`ld_wallet.py`, `ld_sim.py`, `ld_report.py`), branch `research/overnight-1117`, local commits only.
- **Scenarios:** `live` (book +9 ms; post +55 ms in a calm book / +190 ms after a book move; cancel +90 ms, never before order-on-book + 90) primary, `dublin` (+9 / +22 / +21) beside it.
- **Fills:** pess queue (behind all displayed size at our price), strict fill model, settle-only P&L. Late mid markouts are survivorship-biased, so settlement is the measure.

## 1. Verdict
1. **No. At `live` speed no cell has a settle interval above zero on dev (0 of 64), and the pre-registered primary fails the 11-17 holdout:** `fav|60|9700|drop2`, −0.32¢/share [−3.32, +1.87].
2. **A joined bid at 95–99¢ sits behind a large queue:** median ~2,000 shares with 60 s left, ~30–58k with 15–30 s left. Our orders fill mainly when the favourite weakens and price comes back through our bid. Only 21–32% of orders see their queue fully traded, yet 31–63% fill.
3. **The tail decides it.** Favourite losses after our fill are rare (2.5–4% of fills), but one costs ~$4.90 on 5 shares, about 50–100 winning fills' worth (+5 to +1¢ each). Over three dev days the losses cancel the gains in every cell.
4. **Fills in the last 10 s won every time (+1.7¢/share) but are very rare**, ~50 fills a day across all cells (queue ~58k shares). The 15–30 s cells "pass" the holdout only because 11-17 had no late upset in those fills; on dev the same cells were negative.
5. **Worth at our size:** with ≤ 10 shares in ~half the windows, the best dev cell (+0.72¢, fill 0.35) is ≈ +$3.6/day point estimate. The holdout was ≈ −$1.3/day, so **≈ $0 ± a few $/day**: not worth building. Our losing leg would mostly expire worthless either way.

**Reading:**
- 0xb1d304 buys favourites at 98–99¢ in size (tens to hundreds of shares) through the last ~3 minutes, not just the last seconds.
- Its edge holds out of sample, but it is mostly a **taker** edge: paying the ask with ~2 minutes left. Its maker fills, the part comparable to "late dump", are breakeven, with intervals around zero in-sample and out.
- When it joins as a maker it sits behind 1,500–2,000 displayed shares and still fills. That's consistent with having been in the queue for a long time, or with fills on the way down (adverse), which our simulation shows dominate for a newcomer.
- **Copyable by us?** The maker part: no. A participant landing in 55–190 ms behind all displayed size gets the adverse fills (§3). The taker part is a different trade (buy the favourite's ask with ~2 minutes left). It is a directional favourite-buying edge, not speed; it wasn't tested here and would need its own pre-registration.

## 3. Rule results
**Rule:**
- When, in the last T seconds, one side's best bid is ≥ X, post-only BUY 5 of that side at its best bid (= sell 5 of the other side at 1 − bid).
- One order per window. Rest to the close (`none`), or pull when the bid falls 2 ticks (`drop2`).
- Placebo: buy the other side.

All 64 cells × 2 scenarios × dev and holdout: `~/overnight-wt/out/ld/ld_dev.md`, `ld_holdout.md` (with day CIs, shares/day, queue columns).

## 4. Candidate
**None.**
- No cell has an interval above zero at `live` speed on dev, and the frozen primary failed the holdout.
- If the bot must do something with a losing leg below the 5-share minimum, holding it to expiry costs nothing extra.
- The one shape that looked good (fills with ≤ 10 s left, +1.7¢, 0 losses) fills ~5% of the time at our queue position. Even at 10 shares in every second window that is ≈ 10 × 144 × 0.05 × 1.7¢ ≈ $1/day, with an unknown late-upset tail.
- **Separate, untested lead:** 0xb1d304's taker edge (buy the 98–99¢ favourite at its ask with ~2 minutes left, +1.4 / +2.4¢/$ in and out of sample). It is a different rule from this one and would need its own frozen test.
